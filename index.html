Is mass always constant?

Section 6.1

Choose a system and follow its mass. If nothing enters or leaves the system, its mass stays the same.

The flask

The system (dashed outline)

Isolated system: its objects interact only with each other; nothing enters or leaves.

Constancy of mass: the mass of an isolated system stays the same.

When mass does cross the boundary, keep track of it:

mi + Δm = mf

The bar chart keeps track of the mass: the left bar plus the middle bar equals the right bar.

mi + Δm = mf

Mass is a conserved quantity: the fridge loses exactly what the bag gains. For the system “bag + fridge”, Δm = 0.

Linear momentum is a vector

Section 6.2

Momentum is mass times velocity. It points the same way as the velocity, so it has components, and their signs matter.

Graph view 100%

p = mv (6.1)

Unit: kg·m/s. p points the same way as v.

A system’s momentum is the vector sum of its objects’ momenta:

pnet = m1v1 + m2v2 + ⋯

A skydiver falling straight down (press ↓): px = 0 and py = −mv.

Two rollerbladers push off

Example 6.1

Jen and David start at rest and push off each other. Jen travels 3.0 m. How far does David travel in the same time?

The system is both people. The vertical forces cancel and friction is small, so the system is isolated.

0 + 0 = mJvJfx + mDvDfx

No forces were needed: momentum constancy works even when the pushes are not constant.

Section 6.3

A force acting for a time interval gives the ball an impulse, and the impulse changes the ball’s momentum. Watch the shaded area grow.

How the force changes

J = Fav(tf − ti) (6.5)

pf − pi = ΣJ (6.6)

Unit: N·s = kg·m/s, the same as momentum. A small force for a long time can give the same impulse as a large force for a short time.

Abrupt stop in a car

Example 6.2

A person moving at 16 m/s is stopped by an air bag in 0.20 s, or by the dashboard in 0.02 s. The momentum change is the same; the force is not.

The dashboard stops the person in 0.02 s.

The person is the system. Solve the impulse-momentum equation for the force:

Fon P x = m(vfx − vix)tf − ti

A longer stopping time means a smaller force. That is why air bags save lives.

Why the total momentum stays the same

Figure 6.6

During the collision each cart pushes on the other with an equal and opposite force for the same time. The two impulses cancel.

Bumpers

Newton’s third law: F1 on 2 = −F2 on 1 at every instant, for the same Δt. So

m2(v2f − v2i) = −m1(v1f − v1i)

m1v1i + m2v2i = m1v1f + m2v2f

Internal forces cannot change the momentum of the system. Momentum moves from one cart to the other: it is conserved.

Happy and sad balls

Example 6.3

Two balls of the same mass hit identical boards at the same speed. The sad ball stops; the happy ball bounces back. Which knocks its board over?

Each ball has mass 0.10 kg. A board tips over if it receives more than 0.30 N·s.

The ball is the system; +x points toward the board.

Sad: mvi + Jx = 0

Happy: mvi + Jx = −mvi

Bouncing needs twice the momentum change, so the board pushes twice as hard, and by Newton’s third law so does the ball. Bulletproof vests make bullets embed for this reason.

From stopping distance to stopping time

Examples 6.5 and 6.6

If an object slows down steadily, its stopping time follows from its stopping distance. A longer stopping distance means a smaller force.

Assume a constant acceleration while stopping:

tf − ti = 2(yf − yi)vfy + viy (6.10)

mviy + (NC on B − mg)(tf − ti) = 0

An estimate: a 70 kg cyclist at about 3 m/s hits a pole. Keep one significant digit.

FP on B x = −mvixtf − ti

A skull can fracture at about 1.7 × 10⁴ N/cm².

Jet propulsion: a rocket in space

Section 6.6

Far from Earth there is nothing to push against. The rocket throws fuel out of its nozzle, and the fuel pushes the rocket the other way.

Eject the fuel

Your spacecraft moves along with the rocket, so the rocket and fuel start at rest relative to you.

0 = mRvRx + mFvFx

vRx = −mFmRvFx

The force of the fuel on the rocket is the thrust. Ejecting fuel forward slows the rocket down.
